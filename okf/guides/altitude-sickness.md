# Altitude Sickness in Tibet

Altitude sickness (Acute Mountain Sickness / AMS) is the #1 health concern for Tibet travelers. Lhasa sits at 3,650m (11,975 ft), Everest Base Camp at 5,200m (17,060 ft). Everyone will feel some effect — regardless of age, gender, or fitness.

## AMS Severity Levels

| Level | Prevalence | Symptoms | Action |
|-------|-----------|----------|--------|
| Mild | ~90% | Headache, fatigue, poor sleep, reduced appetite | Rest, hydrate, resolves in 2-3 days |
| Moderate | 5-8% | Vomiting, severe headache, rapid heart rate | Oxygen, possible descent |
| Severe (HAPE/HACE) | <1% | Breathlessness at rest, confusion, pink frothy sputum | Emergency descent, medical care |

Sources: International Society of Mountain Medicine (ISMM); Wilderness Medical Society AMS guidelines.

## Prevention (Proven Methods)

1. **Gradual ascent:** 2-3 days in Lhasa (3,650m) before going higher
2. **Diamox (Acetazolamide):** Start 1-2 days before ascent; 125mg twice daily
3. **Hydration:** 2-3 liters of water daily minimum
4. **No alcohol:** For first 3 days at altitude
5. **Slow pace:** Half your normal speed for everything
6. **Don't shower day 1:** Hot water causes vasodilation, worsens hypoxia

## Danger Signs (Seek Immediate Help)

- Breathlessness that persists at rest
- Blue or purple lips/fingernails
- Coughing up pink, frothy sputum
- Confusion, loss of coordination, inability to walk straight
- These indicate HAPE or HACE — life-threatening, requires immediate descent

## Who Should Not Go to High Altitude

- Uncontrolled severe hypertension
- Severe coronary artery disease
- Severe COPD or uncontrolled asthma
- Pregnancy (mid-to-late term)
- Infants under 2 years
