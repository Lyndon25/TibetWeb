# Is Tibet Safe for Tourists in 2026?

**Quick Answer:** Tibet is one of China's safest travel destinations. Lhasa has a police service station every 300 meters with a 3-5 minute response time, and Tibet has one of the lowest violent crime rates among all Chinese provinces. Zero serious violent incidents against international tourists have been reported in the past decade. The only real concern is altitude sickness (AMS): ~90% of cases are mild and resolve in 2-3 days; severe AMS (HAPE/HACE) affects under 1% of travelers and is fully preventable with proper acclimatization.

Sources: Tibet Public Security Bureau statistics; International Society of Mountain Medicine (ISMM) AMS prevalence studies; TibetRide safety records 2016-2026.

## Five Safety Dimensions

### 1. Crime & Personal Safety
- Police service stations at almost every major Lhasa intersection
- 3-5 minute response time, 24/7 patrols
- One of China's lowest violent crime rates
- Safe to walk at night in Lhasa

### 2. Altitude Sickness (The Real Concern)
- **Mild (90%):** Headache, fatigue — resolves in 2-3 days
- **Moderate (5-8%):** Vomiting, severe headache — needs oxygen
- **Severe (<1%):** HAPE/HACE — requires immediate descent
- **Prevention:** Gradual ascent, Diamox, hydration, no alcohol first 3 days

### 3. Road Safety
- Mountain roads with switchbacks and landslide risk
- Weather changes rapidly; snow possible even in summer
- Recommend local drivers over self-driving for first-timers
- Qinghai-Tibet Railway is extremely safe with onboard oxygen

### 4. Political & Travel Restrictions
- Tibet Travel Permit is a routine border region management system, similar to India's PAP/RAP
- Temporary road closures happen during major events or severe weather — not targeting tourists
- Standard rules: don't photograph military facilities, respect religious sites

### 5. Solo Female Travel
- One of Asia's most female-friendly destinations
- No street harassment culture
- Many solo female backpackers at Lhasa hostels
- Police stations everywhere add safety

## Who Should Reconsider?

- Severe cardiovascular disease patients
- Severe respiratory conditions (e.g., uncontrolled asthma)
- Women in mid-to-late pregnancy
- Infants under 2 years (ear pressure + temperature regulation)
